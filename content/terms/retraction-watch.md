A blog founded in 2010 by journalists Adam Marcus and Ivan Oransky that tracks and investigates retractions of scientific papers, run under the nonprofit Center for Scientific Integrity. By reporting individual retractions and aggregating them into a public database, it made the retraction record visible at a scale that no journal or index provides.

## Details

- First-wave new entry (D-5): draft awaiting maintainer review
- The Retraction Watch Database, the largest public index of retractions, was acquired by Crossref and is openly available through the Crossref API; the blog continues its investigative reporting
- Its investigations have exposed paper mills, editor manipulation, and citation gaming, feeding both journalism (partnerships with Science and other outlets) and research-integrity research
- Together with PubPeer it forms the public accountability layer of the scholarly record, complementing slow editorial and institutional processes

> [!WARNING]
> Public retraction databases are still incomplete: many retractions arrive years late, and a large share of problematic papers are corrected or silently withdrawn rather than retracted
