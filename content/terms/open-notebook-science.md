The practice of publishing the research notebook itself, in near real time: experimental plans, raw observations, failed runs and protocol changes, not only the polished final paper. The term was coined and lived by the chemist Jean-Claude Bradley, who ran his lab's synthesis work openly online from 2006, on the argument that the notebook is the actual record of science and the paper is only its summary.

## Details

- Goes a step beyond open data and open access: those release products, open notebook science releases process, including the negatives and dead ends that vanish from published accounts
- Claimed benefits: richer documentation than any paper can carry, third parties catching errors early, and a genuine answer to selective reporting because the record exists before the outcome is known
- In practice it survives mostly in niches (parts of chemistry, citizen science projects, some computational work) because the costs are asymmetric: daily posting effort for diffuse, uncertain benefit

> [!WARNING]
> Real-time openness exposes exactly what secrecy rules were written for: patentable inventions lose novelty protection in many jurisdictions once disclosed, and patent offices grant no reliable general grace period for such disclosures
