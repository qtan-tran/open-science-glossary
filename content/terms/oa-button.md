A browser tool, launched in 2013 by students (Joseph McArthur and David Carroll) and run by open infrastructure nonprofit OA.Works, that helps a reader who has just hit a paywall in two directions at once: it searches for a free legal copy of the article, and if none exists it sends an automated request for open access to the author, who receives a polite email with a one-click way to deposit the paper in a repository.

## Details

- Second-wave new entry (approved 2026-11-20): draft awaiting maintainer review
- Founded as an advocacy instrument: the stream of "I need this to study and cannot afford it" requests is collected as evidence of demand, turning individual frustration into visible, quotable data about access barriers
- The help-me-find-a-copy function overlaps with Unpaywall, but the request function is unique in kind: it works on articles that are closed today and mobilises the only people who can legally open them
- Typically resolves green: the requested version is the author's accepted manuscript, self-archived in a repository, not the publisher's PDF

> [!WARNING]
> Requests only help when the author reacts, has the manuscript, and is allowed to deposit it: rights retention policies and funder mandates are what turn individual author goodwill into a reliable system
