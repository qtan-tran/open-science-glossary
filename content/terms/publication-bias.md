The systematic tendency for studies with positive, "significant" results to be published preferentially, while studies with negative or null results remain unpublished (the "file drawer problem"). Because journals, reviewers and authors all favour novel positive findings, the published literature exaggerates effect sizes and hides the true base of evidence.

## Details

- Related biases compound it: outcome reporting bias (selective reporting of measured outcomes) and citation bias (positive results are also cited more)
- Detectable statistically in a research field via funnel-plot asymmetry and related tests in meta-analysis
- Countermeasures: pre-registration, registered reports, mandates to publish results of all funded studies, and trial registries with legally required summary posting

> [!WARNING]
> Publication bias corrupts meta-analyses too: if the input studies are a biased sample, pooling them yields a precise but inflated estimate
