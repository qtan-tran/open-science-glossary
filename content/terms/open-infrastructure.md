The shared services, software and standards that scholarly communication runs on (repositories, registry and identifier systems, publishing platforms, discovery indexes), governed as a commons: open source or open access, community accountable, and not fully controlled by any single commercial actor. The UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science names open infrastructures as one of the pillars of open science and asks states to support them.

## Details

- Typical examples in this glossary: Open Journal Systems as publishing infrastructure, Zenodo as repository infrastructure, Crossref and DataCite as registry infrastructure, DOAJ and Unpaywall as discovery infrastructure
- The decisive property is governance, not only licensing: who controls the roadmap, the data and the exit options when a service shuts down
- Sustainability is the standing weak point: most open infrastructure depends on a small number of institutions, funders and grants

> [!WARNING]
- "Open infrastructure" is sometimes claimed by commercial services with open APIs but closed governance and closed data; check ownership, exit clauses and bulk export rights before relying on the label
