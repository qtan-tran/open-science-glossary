SANLiC (the South African National Library and Information Consortium) is the consortium that negotiates journal packages and transformative ("read-and-publish") agreements on behalf of South African higher-education institutions, giving the country's universities collective negotiating power they would not have individually.

## Details

- **Function:** SANLiC centralizes license negotiation with major publishers, which matters in a national market where individual universities otherwise face package pricing alone.
- **Open access:** Its read-and-publish agreements let South African affiliated authors publish open access in covered journals without paying individual APCs, funded collectively through the agreement fee.
- **Significance:** SANLiC is a frequently cited example from the Global South, showing that consortial action is a critical lever for research-producing countries outside the big OECD markets.

> [!NOTE]
> Together with CLOSA (a separate South African initiative maintaining a list of accredited local journals) and LIASA (the professional library association), SANLiC is only one, the negotiating one, of South Africa's library-sector bodies.
