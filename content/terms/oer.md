Teaching, learning and research materials in any medium or format that reside in the public domain or have been released under an open licence, so that they can be copied, used, adapted and redistributed by others at no cost. The UNESCO OER Recommendation (2019), the only global standard-setting instrument in this field, frames OER as a means to support quality education and the UN Sustainable Development Goal 4.

## Details

- The characteristic "5R" activities enabled by open licences: retain, reuse, revise, remix and redistribute
- Common licence choices: CC BY and CC BY-SA; the "openness" question turns on whether derivatives and commercial reuse are allowed
- Distinct from "free to view" course content: without licence rights to adapt and redistribute, material is not OER

> [!WARNING]
> OER is about learning materials, OA is about research literature: the terms overlap in spirit and licence practice but answer different questions
