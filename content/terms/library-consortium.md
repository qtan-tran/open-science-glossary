A library consortium is an alliance of libraries (often all the academic libraries of a country or region) that pool budgets, negotiate licenses, and coordinate services with scholarly publishers and infrastructure providers collectively instead of institution by institution.

## Details

- **Why consortia matter:** From the 1990s Big Deal era onward, only collective negotiation gave libraries any bargaining power against major publishers; consortia later became the natural counterpart side for national transformative agreements.
- **Today's scope:** Beyond subscriptions, consortia run shared repositories, preservation networks, open-access collective funding (e.g. supporting diamond journals or Open Journals Collective-type memberships), and shared publishing platforms.
- **Examples:** Large national or multinational consortia include Couperin (France), SANLiC (South Africa), and Jisc's negotiation function (UK); similar bodies exist in most countries.

> [!WARNING]
> Because consortia negotiate in private, their agreements (including read-and-publish details) are exactly the kind of contract information that open-cost and OpenAPC initiatives push publishers to disclose.
