The quantitative study of written publications and their citation patterns: counting papers, citations, journals and collaboration networks to measure the productivity or impact of researchers, institutions and countries. The term was coined by Alan Pritchard in 1969; the field supplies the data and indicators on which much research assessment runs.

## Details

- First-wave new entry (D-5): draft awaiting maintainer review
- Classic objects: citation counts, the Journal Impact Factor, the h-index, and journal or country rankings
- Related families: scientometrics (science indicators more broadly) and informetrics (the whole information literature, including altmetrics)
- Bibliometric facts are descriptions of the citation record, not measures of scientific quality; using them as proxies is a policy choice with documented failure modes

> [!WARNING]
> Citation behaviour differs massively across fields: raw bibliometric numbers are meaningless without field normalisation, and indicator gaming (citation cartels, salami slicing) corrupts them further
