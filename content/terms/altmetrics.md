Metrics of scholarly impact built on the social web: mentions, saves, discussions and citations of research outputs in social media, reference managers, Wikipedia, policy documents and news, rather than in the citation index alone. The 2010 altmetrics manifesto proposed them both as faster, broader signals and as a way to give credit to outputs that citations miss, such as data sets and blog posts.

## Details

- First-wave new entry (D-5): draft awaiting maintainer review
- The manifesto's two claims: track new scholarship on the web, and speed (days instead of years for a first impact signal)
- Commercial and public aggregators collect the raw events; unlike the Journal Impact Factor, altmetrics are computed at the level of single articles and other products
- Value depends on the audience: high Twitter attention often tracks press coverage rather than scientific use

> [!WARNING]
> Easily gamed and heavily skewed: altmetrics count attention, not quality, and attention to a retracted paper can produce impressive altmetric scores
