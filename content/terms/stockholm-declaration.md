The Stockholm Declaration (2025) is a researcher-led call for fundamental reform of scholarly publishing, including stronger research integrity, reduced reliance on publication metrics, and greater support for researcher-led, non-profit publishing. It was published open access in Royal Society Open Science and signed by researchers, editors, and science commentators worldwide.

## Details

- **Diagnosis:** The declaration argues the current publishing system misaligned incentives: prestige concentrates in a small number of very profitable journals, peer review is unpaid and overloaded, and career evaluation rewards glossy placements over rigor.
- **Demands:** Support for researcher-driven publishing (diamond and community-owned journals), decisive reform of research assessment, decisive protection of research integrity over publication throughput, and libraries moving their budgets out of commercial journal packages.
- **Flagship suggestions:** Empowering established open infrastructure (e.g. OpenAIRE-style intermediaries) and turning the screws on the prestige economy by refusing purely metric-based judgments.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not confuse this 2025 declaration with the 2020 "Stockholm Declaration on Open Abstracts" (a call for publishers to release abstracts openly): the two statements share a city, and team names occasionally collide, but they cover different topics.
