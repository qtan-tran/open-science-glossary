A campaign, produced by a coalition of scholarly communication organisations, that teaches researchers to evaluate a journal or publisher before submitting instead of trusting impressive-sounding websites. Its instrument is a plain checklist: Is the journal upfront about fees? Can you contact the publisher? Are the editorial board members real and aware they are listed? Is the journal indexed where it claims? A journal that fails the checks should not receive the manuscript.

## Details

- Second-wave new entry (approved 2026-11-20): draft awaiting maintainer review
- Founded in the mid-2010s in response to the growth of deceptive publishing, and maintained by a cross-sector committee of libraries, publishers, funders and integrity organisations
- Works as harm reduction for the author side of the predatory journal problem: it targets the moment of decision, before a paper, a fee or a copyright is surrendered
- Pairs naturally with the whitelist approach of DOAJ: the checklist sharpens judgement, the directory certifies venues that passed review

> [!WARNING]
> The checklist protects the author from bad publishers, not from bad decisions about quality: a legitimate journal can still be slow, expensive or a questionable fit for the work
