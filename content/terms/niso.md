NISO (the National Information Standards Organization) is a US-based nonprofit standards body that develops and maintains technical standards and best practices for scholarly publishing, libraries, and information systems. Although anchored in the United States, many NISO standards are used worldwide.

## Details

- **What it standardizes:** Machine-readable formats and workflows of scholarly communication, including article tagging (JATS), journal article version identification (JAV), license and metadata expression, and altmetrics definitions.
- **Process:** Standards are developed by working groups of publishers, librarians, service providers, and researchers, then voted on by members before becoming ANSI/NISO standards.
- **Why it matters for open science:** Shared standards are what make open infrastructure interoperable; without them, content could not be exchanged between repositories, journals, and indexes without loss of metadata.

> [!NOTE]
> NISO should not be confused with COUNTER (Counting Online Usage of NeTworked Electronic Resources), which is the separate UK-based standard for usage statistics.
