Open cultural heritage is the practice of making cultural-heritage material openly accessible and reusable: digitized artworks, manuscripts, photographs, audio, 3D scans, and the descriptive metadata about them, published under open licenses (typically CC0 or CC BY). It is the cultural-sector counterpart of open access in scholarly publishing and is closely associated with the OpenGLAM movement.

## Details

- **What open means here:** The same family of licenses as open access, but with an extra layer of friction: the rights status of an old object, of a photograph of it, and of its metadata are three different legal questions, which is why CC0 is recommended for metadata.
- **Practice:** Museums, archives, and libraries release digital copies of their collections, contribute media to Wikimedia Commons, and aggregate openly licensed records in services such as Europeana, which provides access to digitized material from thousands of European institutions.
- **Open science link:** Digital editions, transcriptions, and structured collection data become raw material for research (digital humanities at scale), and reusable images feed back into public knowledge platforms.

> [!WARNING]
> Open by default is not always appropriate: indigenous cultural protocols and sensitive or sacred material require restrictions and community consent, as captured by the CARE principles and indigenous data sovereignty. Open license choice must respect these boundaries.
