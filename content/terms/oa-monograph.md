An OA monograph is a scholarly book made openly available, normally under a Creative Commons license, with its costs funded in some way other than the reader or library buying every copy. Open access for books has advanced much more slowly than for journals, because the monograph is the gold-standard output in the humanities and social sciences and its economics rest on sales to libraries and individuals rather than on article fees.

## Details

- **Why books lag journals:** In the humanities and social sciences careers are built on books, print runs are small, sales revenue is real but modest, and there is no APC tradition to convert into a gold-model fee. Publishers therefore fear, and studies only partly confirm, that a free online edition cannibalizes sales.
- **Funding models:** Book processing charges (the book-length analogue of an APC), library-collective funding of the Opening the Future type, national OA book funds, and conversion approaches such as Subscribe to Open analogues for books.
- **Mandates:** Funder policy is now decisive. UKRI requires that in-scope monographs and book chapters be open access within twelve months of publication, for outputs published from 1 January 2024, with CC BY the preferred license, making UKRI one of the first large funders to push OA from journals into books.
- **Infrastructure:** OAPEN hosts and disseminates open access books and the Directory of Open Access Books (DOAB) indexes them; COPIM built open-infrastructure tooling for OA book publishing; Open Monograph Press is a free platform for running an OA book imprint.

> [!NOTE]
> Many OA monographs live a double life: an openly licensed digital edition and paid print or enhanced electronic editions sold in parallel. What counts is which version the funder mandate and the citation point to.
