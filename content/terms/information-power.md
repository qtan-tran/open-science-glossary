The Information Power guidance refers to transparency frameworks developed by the consultancy Information Power for cOAlition S, recommending that publishers clearly disclose how article processing charges (APCs) break down across publishing services: editorial work, peer review, platform, technology, and production.

## Details

- **Two frameworks:** The Price Transparency Framework asks publishers to declare which services an APC actually pays for (and the share of each), and the Publishing Services Transparency Framework lists the individual services (e.g. retraction handling, plagiarism screening) that APC pricing should cover.
- **Why cOAlition S backs it:** Plan S pushed open access forward without pushing APC prices down; transparency is the chosen instrument for exposing and comparing publisher costs.
- **Open science value:** If widely adopted, framework-based disclosure allows funders and libraries to negotiate on actual service content rather than brand prestige, and connects APC debates to genuine cost drivers.

> [!NOTE]
> Disclosure under the frameworks is publisher-claimed and framework-dependent, so far from being an audited cost breakdown; it improves comparability, not proof.
