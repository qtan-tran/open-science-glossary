ALPSP (the Association of Learned and Professional Society Publishers) is the international trade association for learned and professional society publishers: nonprofit publishers owned by scholarly societies, university presses, and similar organizations. It provides training, networking, and representational advocacy for this sector.

## Details

- **Who it represents:** Society publishers, the organizations that own journals like the Royal Society or American Chemical Society publications, plus presses and library publishers.
- **Open science position:** ALPSP represents the sector most squeezed by big commercial publishers' transformative agreements, and has publicly raised concerns that rights-retention policies and funder mandates transfer value away from societies that fund their fields' activities through journal revenue.
- **Why it matters:** Because society publishers are simultaneously respected members of the scholarly community and businesses dependent on subscription revenue, ALPSP's position papers articulate the strongest noncommercial objections to fast open-access transitions.

> [!NOTE]
> For open-science advocates, ALPSP position statements are useful as the clearest published articulation of society-publisher concerns, rather than as open-science guidance.
