Research4Life is a public-private partnership that provides free or low-cost access to scholarly journals, books, and databases for institutions in low- and middle-income countries. It is supported by publishers (including Elsevier), UN agencies, universities, and other partners, with the explicit aim of reducing inequalities in access to scientific knowledge.

## Details

- **Programmes:** The partnership aggregates five genre-specific programmes (Hinari for health, AGORA for agriculture, OARE for environmental research, ARDI for industrial development, GOALI for legal information) under one umbrella.
- **Eligibility:** Access tiers depend on country income classification and institutional type, with costs rising as national income rises.
- **Open science debate:** Donated access helps individual researchers but keeps them dependent on publisher licensing rather than moving the system toward open access, so Research4Life is praised as humanitarian relief and criticized as a stabilizer of the commercial order.

> [!NOTE]
> Because eligibility follows World Bank income groups, institutions can lose discounted access when their country crosses an income threshold, creating funding cliffs.
