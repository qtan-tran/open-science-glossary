The UK Biobank is a large-scale biomedical research resource containing genetic, health, lifestyle, and imaging data from around 500,000 volunteer participants in the United Kingdom, recruited between 2006 and 2010. It is widely used by researchers worldwide to study the causes, prevention, and treatment of diseases.

## Details

- **Access model:** Data access is managed (not fully open): researchers apply, describe their project, and accept a material transfer agreement; approved researchers worldwide gain access to de-identified data for their projects.
- **Linked depth:** Genotyping and whole-body imaging, long-linked clinical records (with consent), and long-duration follow-up make it a model for deep, reusable cohort resources.
- **Open science significance:** UK Biobank demonstrates that "as open as possible" need not mean "open to everyone by default"; with sensitive human data, managed openness plus FAIR structuring replaces blanket openness, with data use governance as an explicit feature, not a bug.

> [!WARNING]
> Because aggregate genetics-plus-health data can be identifying, UK Biobank explicitly forbids re-identification attempts, embodying the specific ethics rules that differentiate managed-access datasets from open data.
