A bibliometric indicator that attempts to capture both the productivity and citation impact of a researcher. A researcher has an h-index of n if they have n papers that have each been cited at least n times.

## Details

- Proposed by physicist Jorge Hirsch (2005)
- Widely misused in hiring, promotion, and funding decisions; despite well-documented flaws:
- Disadvantages early career researchers, women, and humanities scholars
- Cannot distinguish self-citations, discipline-specific citation norms, or citations by retracted papers
- DORA and CoARA explicitly discourage using the h-index as a primary indicator
