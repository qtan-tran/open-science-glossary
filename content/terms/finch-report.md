The Finch Report is the short name for "Accessibility, sustainability, excellence: how to expand access to research publications", the 2012 report of a UK government-commissioned working group chaired by Janet Finch. It recommended expanding access to research publications chiefly through gold open access funded by article processing charges.

## Details

- **Key recommendation:** Publicly funded research should be published open access, with the costs met through APCs routed through block grants to universities, rather than relying on subscriptions or green self-archiving alone.
- **Influence:** The report directly shaped the 2012 RCUK open-access policy and the long-term direction of UK open-access policy, including UKRI's later requirements.
- **Criticism:** Advocates of green open access and non-APC models argued the report privileged publisher interests, underestimated repositories, and locked public money into hybrid APCs.

> [!NOTE]
> The Finch Report is a useful reference point because later UK policy debates, and the active role of Jisc and UKRI, can all be read as attempts to fix or reverse its APC-first logic.
