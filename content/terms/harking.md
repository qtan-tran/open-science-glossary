The undisclosed practice of presenting post-hoc hypotheses as if they had been formulated before data collection began; making exploratory findings appear confirmatory. Coined by Norbert Kerr (1998).

## Details

- A major driver of the replication crisis: HARKed findings look statistically robust but are actually capitalising on chance
- HARKing inflates effect sizes and reduces reproducibility
- Registered Reports are designed specifically to prevent HARKing: hypotheses are locked in before data collection

> [!WARNING]
> Distinct from post-hoc exploration, which is legitimate science as long as it is clearly labelled as exploratory
