The ongoing finding, across many scientific disciplines, that a significant proportion of published research results cannot be successfully replicated when independent researchers attempt to reproduce them.

## Details

- Crystallised in psychology: the Reproducibility Project (OSC, 2015) replicated only ~39% of 100 psychology studies
- Also documented in medicine, economics, ecology, neuroscience, and beyond
- Contributing factors: HARKing, p-hacking, publication bias, small sample sizes, selective reporting, and insufficient data sharing
- “Replication Games”: organised competitions (e.g. I4R) in which multiple teams simultaneously replicate the same paper
