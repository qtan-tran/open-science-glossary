The clause in a traditional publication contract under which the author signs the copyright in the manuscript over to the publisher, usually in exchange for publication. From that point the publisher, not the author, owns the text and decides who may copy, distribute, text-mine or re-use it, and the author's own re-use rights depend on whatever permissions the contract carves out.

## Details

- Typical carve-out: the right to self-archive the accepted manuscript (the basis of the green OA layer) and to reuse parts in future work
- Alternatives that keep more rights with the author: author addenda, licence-to-publish contracts, and outright refusal in favour of open licences
- Copyright transfer is the legal backbone of the subscription model: it gives the publisher the exclusive rights that subscription and hybrid sales monetise

> [!WARNING]
> Signing is often presented as unavoidable boilerplate; clauses vary widely between publishers and can be negotiated or amended, and public funders increasingly require authors to retain rights instead
