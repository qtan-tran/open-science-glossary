An archive created, owned and governed by the community it documents, rather than by an institution acting on its behalf: collections of photographs, documents, recordings or artefacts assembled by neighbourhood groups, migrant communities, faith groups, or social movements to preserve their own memory on their own terms. The decisive features are community control over what is collected, who may access it, and how it is interpreted.

## Details

- Motivated in part by exclusion: mainstream memory institutions historically collected little about marginalised groups and described them in hostile categories
- Digital tools lower the cost of self-archiving but raise new governance questions: hosting on commercial platforms can quietly relocate control out of the community, and digitisation grant conditions often demand open licences that conflict with community norms
- Borders the Indigenous data sovereignty claims: for Indigenous collections, authority to control extends to data governance, not only custody of physical items

> [!WARNING]
> "Community consent" is not a checkbox: an open licence chosen by a project partner is not the same as a licence chosen by the community whose memory is at stake
