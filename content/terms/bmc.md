BioMed Central (BMC) is an open-access publisher founded in London in 2000, one of the first companies to build an entire portfolio of open-access journals funded by article processing charges. It was acquired by Springer in 2013 and is now part of Springer Nature, where its name survives as the BMC-series of journals plus other open-access titles.

## Details

- **Historical importance:** Alongside the Public Library of Science (PLoS), BMC proved from the early 2000s that a commercial fully-open publisher could scale, directly pioneering the APC-funded gold open access model.
- **The BMC series:** Its flagship BMC-branded megajournals (BMC Medicine, BMC Biology and many field-specific ones) accept methodologically sound research regardless of perceived novelty, an editorial philosophy that prefigured today's megajournal movement.
- **Data-sharing policies:** BMC journals adopted early mandatory data-availability statements and trial-registration requirements, making the portfolio a reference point for open editorial policies.

> [!NOTE]
> BMC's 2013 acquisition into Springer Nature illustrates the consolidation critique of open access: APC-funded open publishers were absorbed by the same commercial groups they positioned themselves against.
