An umbrella concept for movements and practices that aim to make scientific knowledge openly available, accessible and reusable for everyone, and to open the processes of creating and evaluating that knowledge to collaborators beyond the traditional research community. The UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science (2021) defines it as an "inclusive construct" spanning open access, open data, open educational resources, open engagement of societal actors, and other components, combined with commitments to equity and diversity in knowledge production.

## Details

- First-wave new entry (D-5): draft awaiting maintainer review; this entry is the anchor that the other "open X" entries link back to
- Core strands commonly distinguished: open access (publications), open data and open methods, open peer review, open educational resources, and participation of societal actors such as citizen scientists
- UNESCO's definition couples openness with the goals of increasing research quality, collaboration, and the sharing of knowledge beyond the walls of academia
- Debated criticisms concern the risk of a "publish or perish" open-science industry, the dominance of English-language infrastructure, and unequal capacity to be open across world regions

> [!WARNING]
> Open science is not the same as open access: publication access is one strand among several, and "open" does not remove the need for quality control, ethics review, or rights management
