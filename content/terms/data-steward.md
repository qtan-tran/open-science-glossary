A professional role, increasingly institutionalised in universities and research institutes, responsible for supporting researchers through the data life cycle: writing and executing data management plans, choosing repositories and licences, applying the FAIR principles, handling privacy and ethics constraints, and making data findable when a project ends. Unlike a data manager, a steward's remit is the whole life cycle and its connection to policy, not a single project's files.

## Details

- Second-wave new entry (approved 2026-11-20): draft awaiting maintainer review
- The role grew with data management mandates: national funders and the European Commission require data management plans and long-term availability, and researchers needed trained people between the policy and the lab bench
- Disciplines and institutions differ sharply in where stewards sit (library, faculty, IT), and communities of practice have emerged to align curricula
- Stewardship of data about Indigenous peoples is bounded by Indigenous data sovereignty: in those settings the steward shares authority over access decisions with the community concerned

> [!WARNING]
> A data steward cannot rescue poor data practices after the fact; the leverage is early involvement, ideally at proposal stage when ethics, consent and budgets are set
